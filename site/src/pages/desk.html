The desk

One file open, every date in view.

The desk is where a matter lives from the day the papers arrive to the day the advocate approves the brief. Three screens, one idea: time is the only thing that gets colour.

OnePapers arrive

The clerk drops in a photo of the cheque, the return memo and the notice.

TwoFacts appear

Each field shows the paper and the spot it came from. Missing dates are asked for.

ThreeClocks start

Four periods, counted by rule. The one that matters shows days left.

FourBrief drafted

A short chronology, every sentence footnoted, any unsupported line removed.

FiveAdvocate approves

By name, after checking. The approval is recorded. Nothing is filed or sent.

Screen one

The docket

Every matter, ordered by what runs out first. A matter whose window closes in five days sits above one with a month to go, whatever order they arrived in. The colour is the only signal: blue is calm, amber is running short, red is urgent, grey has ended.

Screen two

The matter

A matter is the long table: five dates as pins, four clocks as bars. Slide the day the notice was sent and see what happens to the window. Beside it sit the facts, each with its source, and the three papers themselves.

The advocate gets two answers where the law leaves room for doubt: the last day the rule allows, and an earlier date that removes the doubt.

Screen three

The brief

A page the advocate can read in two minutes. Every sentence has a number, every number a source. A sentence without a source never reaches this page. The advocate approves by typing a name, and that approval is the only way a brief leaves draft.

Also on the desk

Two helpers, kept on a short lead.

Take the client’s account by voice

The client speaks in English, Hindi or Punjabi. The desk writes it down, hides names, numbers and addresses, and only then lets a model read it. The original recording stays on the machine.

Ask the file a question

“When does the complaint window close?” An assistant answers from that one matter’s facts and its counted dates, through two read-only tools. It cannot change a fact, count its own date, or see another matter.

Status today: the date engine and these screens are built and tested on the sample matter. Connecting the reading, voice and drafting services to Azure is in progress. The docs page lists what is done and what is not.
