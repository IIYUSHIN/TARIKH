How the clocks are counted

Every rule, its source, and its doubts.

Dates are arithmetic, so Tarikh does them with a written rule engine and not a model. This page lists each rule as it is coded, where it comes from, and how settled it is. If a rule is contested, it says so.

The rule register

What the engine does, rule by rule.

RuleHow it is countedSourceStanding
Cheque validityThree months from the date on the cheque. The period ends on the day before the same date three months later.RBI circular of 4 November 2011; banking convention for the last dayApplied, last day to confirm

More than one presentationA cheque can be presented again within its validity. The desk takes one presentation at a time and says which it used.Case law, still being checkedOpen question

How sure is “sure”? Every source above is a secondary one, such as a published judgment summary or commentary. The primary texts, the Act on India Code, the judgments themselves and the RBI circular, are still to be read and cited line by line. Until that is done, treat each date as a careful first count, not as authority.

Sources also disagree on whether Section 138’s text refers to a six-month or a three-month period for presenting the cheque. Tarikh uses three months, which is what the RBI rule requires, and flags the point.

Worked example. Drawer receives the notice on 3 November. Fifteen days run 4 to 18 November. The complaint window opens on 19 November and closes on 19 December. The cautious date is 18 December.
