01 · Read

Paper in, facts out.

Tarikh reads cheques, return memos, demand notices, postal receipts and tracking printouts. It returns each value with the box it was read from and a confidence score. Low-confidence values are flagged for a person.

  • Return reasons understood. “Funds insufficient”, “payment stopped”, “account closed”, “signature differs”, mapped to how each is usually treated.
  • Missing dates asked for. The day the memo reached the client is on no paper, so Tarikh asks.
  • Duplicates caught. The same cheque uploaded twice opens one matter, not two.

02 · Count

Four windows, one rule engine.

Cheque validity, the 30-day notice, the drawer’s 15 days and the one-month complaint window are counted in plain, tested code. Where the law leaves room for argument, Tarikh shows both readings.

  • The legal last day and the cautious one, side by side.
  • Weekends flagged. Court holidays and delay are left to the advocate, and said so.
  • A public rule register. Every rule, its source and how settled it is.

03 · Draft

Drafts you can check line by line.

A drafting model writes only from facts that trace to a paper or to the advocate’s own entry. Each fact in the draft carries its source. Anything else is removed before a person sees it.

  • Demand notice under Section 138(b).
  • Complaint with synopsis, list of documents and the contacts affidavit.
  • Applications under Sections 143A and 147, and for condonation of delay.
  • Evidence affidavit under Section 145.

04 · Diary

The next tarikh, for every matter.

Hearings, statutory deadlines and settlement instalments on one calendar. After each hearing, record what happened in a line. Tarikh counts adjournments and shows which matters are stalling.

  • Black for hearings, red for deadlines. Sundays in red, as on the wall.
  • Cause-list import from the case number, planned with the eCourts services.

05 · Settle

Settlement, priced and tracked.

Compounding costs depend on the stage the case has reached. Tarikh prices a settlement at today’s stage, drafts the joint application and tracks each instalment against the hearing dates.

06 · Speak

The client tells it in their own language.

A client can speak in Hindi, Punjabi or English. Tarikh writes it down, masks names, numbers and addresses, and only then lets a model help turn it into a statement for the advocate.

  • Personal details masked first. Before any drafting model reads the account.
  • The recording stays with the chambers. Only the text moves on.

07 · Ask

Ask the file. Only the file.

An assistant answers questions about one matter. It has two tools, both read-only: the matter’s facts and its counted dates. It cannot change a fact, count a date itself, open another matter or search the web.

ADVOCATE

Can we still file in Kalra? And what if the court doesn’t sit on the last day?

TARIKH

Yes. The complaint window opened on 19 November and the last day by the rule is 19 December 2026, a Saturday. Counting from the day payment fell due gives 18 December, a Friday, which leaves no room for argument. Whether the court sits on the 19th is for you to confirm.
