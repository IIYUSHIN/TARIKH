Supreme Court · 25 September 2025

The 2025 directions, in plain language.

In Sanjabij Tari v. Kishore S. Borcar (2025 INSC 1158) the Court set out how trial courts must handle Section 138 cases. High Courts and district courts were told to put them in place by 1 November 2025.

SUMMONS

Served by hand and electronically

Besides the usual modes, the complainant may serve summons (dasti), and electronic service is used where rules allow. At filing, the complainant gives the accused’s email, mobile and messaging details on affidavit.

PAYMENT

Pay early, online

District courts are to set up QR or UPI payment. The summons tells the accused they can pay the cheque amount at the start, after which the case may be compounded or closed.

SYNOPSIS

A one-page summary in every complaint

A synopsis in a set format sits right after the index, before the complaint itself, with the cheque, dishonour and notice particulars.

PROCEDURE

No summons before cognizance

Trial courts need not issue notice to the accused before taking cognizance. Interim deposit under Section 143A can be ordered early.

COURTS

Physical courts after service

Once summons is served, cases move to physical courts. Evening courts are to get realistic money limits.

DASHBOARDS

Pendency watched every month

District judges in Delhi, Mumbai and Calcutta keep dashboards of pendency, disposal, settlement and average adjournments by stage.

Compounding costs

The later the payment, the higher the cost.

The Court revised the costs first set in Damodar S. Prabhu (2010). If the accused pays before defence evidence, the case can be compounded without cost. After that, a percentage of the cheque amount is added.

Before defence evidence0%
After defence evidence, before the trial court’s judgment5%
Before a Sessions Court or High Court, in revision or appeal7.5%
Before the Supreme Court10%

Key sections

The Act, in seven lines.

  • Section 138Dishonour of a cheque for insufficient funds, or because it exceeds the arrangement, is an offence once the notice-and-payment conditions are met.
  • Section 139The court presumes the cheque was given for a debt or liability, unless the accused proves otherwise.
  • Section 141When a company issues the cheque, those in charge of its business can also be liable.
  • Section 142Only the payee or holder can complain, in writing, within a month of the cause of action, before the court with jurisdiction.
  • Section 143AThe trial court may order the accused to pay interim compensation of up to 20 percent of the cheque amount.
  • Section 145The complainant’s evidence may be given by affidavit.
  • Section 147Every offence under the Act is compoundable.
