- Section 138, Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881: it is an offence when a cheque, given to discharge a legally enforceable debt or liability, is returned unpaid because the account has insufficient funds or the amount exceeds the arrangement with the bank. Punishment: imprisonment up to two years, or fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both. (Section 138 text as reproduced by devgan.in and SCC Online.)
- Three conditions in the provisos: (a) the cheque was presented to the bank within three months from the date on which it was drawn, or within its validity period, whichever is earlier (RBI has set cheque validity at three months since 1 April 2012); (b) the payee made a written demand for the amount within 30 days of receiving information from the bank that the cheque was returned; (c) the drawer failed to pay within 15 days of receiving that notice. (eCourts judicial training note; iPleaders.)
- Section 142(1)(b): the complaint must be made within one month of the date the cause of action arises, which is the day after the 15 days end. The court may take cognizance later if the complainant shows sufficient cause for the delay. (Corrida Legal; iPleaders.)
- Section 142(2): the complaint is tried by the court within whose local jurisdiction the payee's bank branch lies when the cheque was delivered for collection through an account, or the drawer's branch when it was presented otherwise. (eCourts judicial training note.)
- Section 143: these cases are tried summarily. (eCourts judicial training note.)
- Section 147: offences under the Act are compoundable. (Bhatt and Joshi Associates.)
