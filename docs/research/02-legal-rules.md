# Section 138 rules and where each comes from (draft, step 13 in progress)

Read on 20 November 2026. **Nothing here has yet been checked against the primary texts** (India Code for the Act, the Supreme Court's own
judgment PDFs, the RBI circular). The sources below are reproductions, summaries and reports. Step 13 is not finished until every row
says "primary read".

| No. | Rule | Source read | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The cheque must be presented to the bank within six months of its date or its validity period, whichever is earlier. Since 1 April 2012 the RBI validity is three months from the date of the instrument, so three months applies. | Section 138 proviso (a) on [Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/1823824); RBI rule as reported by [Deccan Herald on a Karnataka High Court order](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/hc-quashes-conviction-in-cheque-bounce-case-4062502) and bank notices | Secondary. RBI circular of 4 November 2011 not yet read. |
| 2 | The written demand notice goes within 30 days of receiving information from the bank that the cheque is returned. | Section 138 proviso (b), same page | Secondary |
| 3 | The drawer has 15 days from receiving the notice to pay. The days run from receipt, not dispatch. | Section 138 proviso (c); [C.C. Alavi Haji v. Palapetty Muhammed](https://advocategandhi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/C.C.-Alavi-Haji-v.-Palapetty-Muhammed-Anr.pdf) on receipt versus giving | Judgment excerpt read, not the whole judgment |
| 4 | The complaint is made within one month of the date the cause of action arises under 138(c). | Section 142(1)(b) as quoted in several High Court orders | Secondary |
| 5 | The first day is excluded in counting. For the complaint month, the cause-of-action date is excluded. | [Econ Antri v. Rom Industries, 26 August 2013](https://www.legalauthority.in/judgement/m-s-econ-antri-ltd-vs-m-s-rom-industries-ltd-6055), reaffirming Saketh India; Section 9 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 | Summary read. The Court's own final-day date is inferred, not stated. |
| 6 | The same exclusion applies to the 30-day notice window: the day information is received is not counted. | A High Court order reported on [Tax Management India](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_case_laws.asp?ID=356937) | A High Court only. Supreme Court authority not yet found. |
| 7 | The court may take cognizance of a late complaint if there was sufficient cause. | Proviso to Section 142(1)(b), as described in several High Court orders | Secondary |

## Left to the advocate, never to the engine
Condonation of delay, court holidays, whether notice was really served (presumption of service by registered post is a matter
for evidence), and all legal advice.

## Open questions, each to be settled with a primary source before the engine is written
1. How the first day is counted for the cheque's three-month validity. The RBI wording is "within three months from the date of
   such instrument". Whether 20 September's cheque is good through 19 December or 20 December is not yet settled.
2. What "one month" means when the target day does not exist, for example a cause of action on 30 January. Section 3(35) of the
   General Clauses Act defines a month as a calendar month, but the month-end rule is not yet read.
3. Second and later presentations of the same cheque. One commercial page says a fresh 30 days begins after each dishonour. That is
   not an adequate source. A Supreme Court authority is needed before Tarikh handles more than one presentation, or the scope
   statement in step 14 says it does not.
